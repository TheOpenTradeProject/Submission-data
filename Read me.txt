THE OPEN TRADE PROJECT
Modeling Value and Uncertainty in NFL Draft Trades
Open-source data for the MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference Research Papers
competition (Football track).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Traditional NFL draft value charts assign a single number to each selection, so
every trade resolves into a winner and a loser. This project treats a pick's value
as a distribution instead of a point: it takes a published chart as the prior mean
for each pick and estimates, from historical trades, how far realized trade values
spread around that mean.

The five files here are the data behind that analysis: the trade records, the two
charts used as priors, the per-pick deviations recovered from each trade, and the
per-pick error comparison between the two charts.

All data is derived from publicly available NFL draft trade records.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONVENTIONS THAT APPLY TO EVERY FILE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Pick numbering
  Picks are numbered continuously from 1 to 261 across the entire draft, with no
  round boundaries. The last pick of round one is 32 and the first pick of round
  two is 33. This keeps numbering consistent across years, since the number of
  compensatory picks varies from draft to draft.

Compensatory picks
  Treated as regular picks, including Resolution JC-2A selections.

Forfeited picks
  Retained as "ghost" selections so that later pick numbers do not shift. For
  example, Miami forfeited its 2023 first-round pick (20); pick 21 remains 21
  rather than moving up to 20.

What is excluded
  Trades involving players, and trades involving future draft picks. The dataset
  consists exclusively of pick-for-pick swaps within a single draft.

What is not modeled
  Player evaluation and post-draft player outcomes. Only the valuation of picks
  and trades is analyzed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FILES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. all_trades_2002_2025.xlsx
   The trade records (three sheets)

   Sheet "standard"  -- THE DATASET USED IN THE PAPER
     477 trades, 2002 through 2025, pick-for-pick only. Columns:
       Year              draft year
       Team 1            first team in the transaction
       Pick(s) traded    picks RECEIVED by Team 1
       Team 2            second team in the transaction
       Pick(s) traded2   picks RECEIVED by Team 2
       Pre-draft?        D  = executed during the draft
                         PD = executed before the draft began

     The analysis in the paper uses the 2012-2025 subset: 318 trades, chosen
     because the Rich Hill chart was built to reflect the 2011 CBA and a
     comparison against Jimmy Johnson is only fair over the post-CBA period.

     Note on direction: both pick columns list what each team transacts. A pick in
     the "Team 1" column was sent by Team 1, and vice versa.

     Relocated franchises appear in combined form, e.g.
     OAK/LAV for Oakland / Las Vegas.

2. JJ_RH_trade_charts.xlsx
   The two charts used as prior means, side by side. Sheet "Trade charts".
     Pick number           1 to 261
     Jimmy Johnson value   the 1990s chart, maximum 3,000 at pick 1
     Rich Hill value       the 2017 chart, maximum 1,000 at pick 1

   IMPORTANT: the two columns are different arbitrary currencies. They must not
   be differenced, ratioed, or compared directly. Comparing the charts requires
   a scale-invariant metric, which is why the file in item 5 reports a RELATIVE
   error rather than an absolute one.

3. Observations_JJ_2012_2025.xlsx
4. Observations_RH_2012_2025.xlsx
   The per-pick deviations recovered from the trades - item 3 using the Jimmy
   Johnson chart as the prior, item 4 using Rich Hill. Each file has 15 sheets, one per year.

     Sheet "Combined"   all 1,101 observations, columns Year / Pick / Deviation
     Sheets "2012".."2025"   the same data split by draft year, Pick / Deviation

   A deviation is the difference between a pick's realized transacted value in
   one trade and its prior value from the chart, expressed in that chart's own
   points. Positive means the pick transacted above its chart value.

   These deviations are MODEL-IMPLIED, not directly observed. They are recovered
   by solving the trade parity model, which assumes each trade exchanges equal
   true value and distributes the discrepancy across the picks involved in
   proportion to their prior values. They should be read as a measure of how
   consistent a chart is with observed trade behavior, not as a direct
   measurement of what a pick is worth.

   Both files contain 1,101 observations covering 252 distinct picks. Nine picks
   appear in no qualifying trade over 2012-2025: 1, 2, 19, 177, 223, 253, 254,
   258 and 260. The two files have identical row counts by year because they
   describe the same trades under two different priors.

5. JJ_RH_RMSE.xlsx
   Per-pick relative root mean square error for both charts, over 2012-2025.
   Sheet "JJ_RH_comp".
     Pick Number
     RRMSE (Jimmy Johnson)
     RRMSE (Rich Hill)

   For pick j, RRMSE is the root mean of the squared relative deviations for
   that pick:

     RRMSE_j = sqrt( (1/n_j) * SUM_i ( (y_ij - mu_j) / mu_j )^2 )

   where n_j is the number of observations for pick j, y_ij is the transacted
   value in observation i, and mu_j is the chart's prior value. Normalizing by
   mu_j makes the measure dimensionless, which is what allows the two charts to
   be compared despite their different scales.

   Lower is better. The Rich Hill chart has the smaller RRMSE on 176 of the 252
   picks that have observations. Both charts fit late-round picks least well.

   The nine picks with no observations show #NUM! rather than a value.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REPRODUCING THE PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The order of operations is:

  1. Take the 2012-2025 pick-for-pick trades from all_trades_2002_2025.xlsx,
     sheet "standard".
  2. Assign each pick its prior value from JJ_RH_trade_charts.xlsx.
  3. Solve the trade parity model for each trade to recover a deviation for
     every pick involved. This reproduces items 3 and 4.
  4. Aggregate the deviations by pick and normalize by the prior value. This
     reproduces item 5.

The MATLAB implementation of steps 3 and 4, and of the kernel-regression
uncertainty estimation that follows, is complete and will be released at the
appropriate stage of the submission.
